`GradientFx` draws a field of soft colour bands that bend and flow through each other under a fine film grain, the look of a blurred mesh gradient behind a hero. It is one small shader, capped at 30 frames a second, that sleeps while it is off screen or in a hidden tab. Children sit on top of it, so the field is the section itself rather than a layer you position. It ships in `@once-ui-system/fx` from 3.1.

```tsx
import { GradientFx } from "@once-ui-system/fx";
```

With no `colors`, the field runs from the page background through your brand to your accent, so it follows the theme, the scheme and any `StyleScope` around it, and redraws when the theme changes.

## Colours

`colors` is a ramp from shadow to highlight, two to six stops. The first stop is the ground the bands rest on. Each stop can be a token (`brand-solid-strong`, `accent-weak`, `page-background`), a custom property (`--my-color`) or any CSS colour.

## Blur and grain

`blur` sets how soft the bands are, from crisp ribbons at `0` to a haze at `1` (default `0.6`). `grain` sets the film grain, from clean at `0` to heavy at `1` (default `0.35`).

## Direction, speed and seed

`angle` turns the bands, in degrees: `0` runs them left to right and a positive angle turns them clockwise (default `-30`). `speed` scales the drift, as a number or `"slow"`, `"medium"` and `"fast"` (0.5, 1 and 2). `seed` picks the field: the same seed draws the same field on every visit and on the server, so a page doesn't change between renders.

## How it works

- **One shader.** A single fragment shader draws the bands from layered noise, warps them so they bend, and adds a fresh grain each frame with a little dither against banding.
- **Cheap to keep on screen.** It renders at most 30 frames a second, pauses when it scrolls out of view or the tab is hidden, and resumes where it left off.
- **Reduced motion.** With `reducedMotion="auto"` (the default) it follows the visitor's setting and holds a still frame. `true` always holds still; `false` always moves.
- **Server and no WebGL.** On the server, and in a browser without WebGL, it draws blurred CSS blobs from the same seed and colours, so the first paint already looks right and the shader fades in over it.
- **Decoration only.** The field is hidden from assistive technology; only its children are read.
