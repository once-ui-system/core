Start from the question the reader brings, not from the data. "How did revenue move this year?" is a line. "Which team has the most open tickets?" is a bar. "What share of sessions came from search?" is a pie, if there are only a few parts. The same rows can answer all three, and each question wants a different chart.

## By question

- **How did it change over time?** [`LineChart`](/data/lineChart). A line says the value moved continuously between readings.
- **Which is bigger?** [`BarChart`](/data/barChart). Lengths from a shared baseline are the easiest thing to compare.
- **Which is bigger, with long names?** `BarChart orientation="horizontal"`. Category names read along the row instead of being cut under the bar.
- **What adds up to the total?** `BarChart stack` or `LineChart stack`. Each part sits on the one before it, so the top is the total.
- **What is each part's share, over time?** `stack="percent"`. Every bar or column runs to 100%, so shares compare across rows.
- **What is each part's share, once?** [`PieChart`](/data/pieChart). Two to five slices. Past that, use a horizontal bar chart.
- **How do a budget and its spend compare?** [`LineBarChart`](/data/lineBarChart). A line for the target, bars for the actuals, on one scale.
- **How is it trending, in a tile or a table cell?** [`Sparkline`](/data/sparkline). The shape only: no axes, legend or card.
- **How close is it to a limit?** [`RadialGauge`](/data/radialGauge) or [`LinearGauge`](/data/linearGauge). One value against a range, such as a quota or a score.

## By data shape

- A date and one or more numbers per row: `LineChart`, or `BarChart` for a handful of periods.
- A category and one number per row: `BarChart`, or `PieChart` for a few parts of a whole.
- A category and several numbers per row: `BarChart` (grouped), or `stack` when the numbers add up.
- A target and an actual per row: `LineBarChart`.
- Readings at uneven intervals: `LineChart xAxisType="number"`.
- A plain array of numbers: `Sparkline`.
- One number: a gauge, or a stat tile with a `Sparkline`.

Every chart but `Sparkline` and the gauges takes the same `data` and `series`, so moving between `LineChart`, `BarChart` and `LineBarChart` is a change of the tag name. See [Data and series](/data/data) for the shape.

## Things to avoid

- **A pie with many slices.** Past five, the small slices are slivers nobody can compare. Keep the top four and group the rest as "Other", or use a horizontal bar chart. The recipe is in [Recipes](/data/recipes#top-n-and-other).
- **A smooth line through jumps.** The default `curve="natural"` claims the value moved between readings. A price tier or a seat count changed in steps: use `curve="step"`.
- **Two scales on one axis.** `LineBarChart` has one y axis. A rate (4%) next to a count (8,000) sits flat on the baseline. Use two charts.
- **Stacking things that don't add up.** Stack revenue by channel, not revenue and margin. The top of a stack should mean "total".
- **A bar chart for a long time series.** Fifty bars become a comb. Use a line, or `barWidth="fill"` if it has to be bars.
- **Colour that only decorates.** A single series doesn't need a hue per bar. Give it the brand (`color: "brand"`) and let the length do the work.
