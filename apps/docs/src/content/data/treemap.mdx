`Treemap` draws one tile per row of `data`, its area the row's value. With `toneKey` each tile is also coloured by a signed number, such as a change since purchase: above zero in the gain colour, below zero in the loss colour, stronger the further from zero. Use it when there are too many parts for a [`PieChart`](/data/pieChart) and their sizes matter more than their order. It takes the card, `title`, `description`, `valueFormat`, `locale`, `tooltip`, `loading`, `error`, `emptyState` and `errorState` the other charts take.

Rows without a positive value are left out, and tiles are laid out largest first so the biggest sit top left. A tile writes its name when it fits and `subKey`'s text when there is room for a second line; its hover card and its accessible name always carry the value, the tone and that text.

## Colour

Without `toneKey` every tile is neutral and area alone carries the data. With it, the strongest tone gets the strongest colour; set `toneMax` to fix that scale instead, so two treemaps compare (`toneMax={0.5}` paints anything at ±50% at full strength). Gains are emerald and losses orange by default, a pair that stays apart for colour-blind readers in light and dark; `gainColor` and `lossColor` take any palette name or CSS colour.
